Is That Crack in Your Boat Cosmetic or Structural?

Toronto Yacht Club · November 20, 2026

Most cracks you find on a fiberglass boat are in the gelcoat, the thin coloured layer on the outside. Those are cosmetic, although they still let water in. A crack is structural when the fiberglass underneath is broken, or when the area around it moves, flexes or sounds hollow.

How to tell

  • Press on it. If the area around the crack flexes or feels soft, the laminate or the core underneath is damaged. A gelcoat crack on solid fiberglass doesn't move.
  • Tap around it with the handle of a screwdriver. Solid laminate sounds sharp. A dull or hollow sound usually means the layers have separated or the core is wet.
  • Look at the pattern. Spider cracks spreading out from a cleat, rail base or other fitting usually mean the hardware is flexing the deck under load. Long cracks along the hull, especially near the keel or the chines, can come from an impact or from the hull working.
  • Look at the depth. If you can see glass fibres, or the crack has an edge you can catch with a fingernail over a wide area, it has gone through the gelcoat.
  • Check the other side. Water stains, rust weeping from screw holes, or a damp spot in the bilge near the crack mean water is getting in.

Why a cosmetic crack still matters

Gelcoat is what keeps water out of the laminate. A crack lets water in, and over an Ontario winter that water freezes and pushes the crack open a little further each time. Around fittings, water can reach the wood core used in many decks and transoms, and wet core rots. A gelcoat crack fixed early is a small job. The same crack left for a few seasons can turn into a core repair, which is a much bigger one. Here is what that repair involves.

When to have it looked at

  • Any crack that flexes, sounds hollow or sits below the waterline.
  • Cracks around the transom, the engine mount or anything else that carries load.
  • Spider cracks that come back after they've been filled.
  • Damage from a dock, a rock or a trailer.

Anything else, keep an eye on it and have it repaired before launch.
